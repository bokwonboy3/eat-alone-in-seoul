How it works

1 · SearchType anything — “hongdae station”, “ramen near gangnam”, a dish, a Korean name — or tap Near me.
2 · CopyTap Copy Korean name, or open the place straight in Naver Map or Kakao Map.
3 · GoPaste into Naver Map. Hours, photos, the queue — and reviews in English if the app is set to English.

What’s on each card

Solo-friendly · N votes — for restaurants: how many Naver visitors voted “good for eating alone” (혼밥하기 좋아요) for that exact place. Real diners, not opinions. A place makes the list when at least 30 visitors voted this and those votes are at least 15% of its most-voted keyword — so a tiny noodle bar and a 5,000-review ramen chain are judged on the same scale.

⭐ Solo favorite — “good for eating alone” is at least 30% of that place’s top keyword. These are the places Koreans actively describe as solo spots, not just places that tolerate one diner.

For cafés the question is different — no café in Seoul turns away a solo customer; what matters is whether it’s a good place to sit alone for a while. So a café’s number combines Naver votes for “good for eating alone”, “good for focusing” and “good for time alone”, plus half-weight for “comfortable seats”, “calm” and “cozy”. It’s a weaker signal than the restaurant one, and we say so.

Koreans say — the top things Korean visitors voted about the place, translated.

Ratings — Naver’s visitor-review score (receipt-verified visits; Naver stopped showing it publicly in 2021, but it still exists) and Kakao Map’s user rating with its vote count, when available. They often disagree — Kakao skews harsher with fewer votes — so read the solo votes first, then the stars.

English menu · Kiosk · English-speaking staff · Menu with pictures — shown when Google reviews written in English mention them for that place. It’s what other foreigners reported, not a guarantee; “reviews” next to the tag reminds you of that. No tag doesn’t mean no English menu — just that nobody mentioned it.

Open now / Closed now · Today’s hours — from Google’s or Kakao Map’s listed opening hours (break times are treated as closed), computed in Seoul time. Holidays and last-order times can differ; when in doubt, check Naver Map.

Expect a wait / Usually no wait · Reservations — waiting is inferred from English Google reviews mentioning a queue (an estimate, not live data); “Reservations” means Google lists the place as reservable — in Korea that usually means Naver or CatchTable booking.

Halal · Vegetarian options — Halal is shown when reviews mention it or the place is a kebab / Turkish / Middle Eastern restaurant; always confirm at the counter. Vegetarian is Google’s own flag or a review mention.

🚇 Nearest station · Exit · walk time — from Kakao Map’s directions data. Exit numbers matter in Seoul: the wrong exit can add ten minutes.

🚫 Closed Mon — days with no listed opening hours. Korean restaurants often close one weekday; check the sign or Naver if it matters.

📊 Busy times — Kakao Map’s hourly visitor counts for today’s weekday, scaled 0–9. A quiet hour is the easiest time to get a seat alone; the tall bars are when you may wait.

Sort — “Most solo” ranks by the share of a place’s votes that are for eating alone (the truest solo signal); “Most votes” by raw count (popularity); “Rating” by the average of Naver, Google and Kakao stars; “Nearest” appears after you tap 📍 Near me.

Want another area or city? Found a wrong name? — Request an area · Report wrong info. Both open a short form on GitHub (no account needed to read; a free one to post). Requests decide what we build next.

Menu & prices — the place’s own listed dishes on Naver, with our English rendering of each name (a translation aid, not the official menu) and a price range. Prices in ₩ (KRW); ₩10,000 ≈ US$7.

Reviews ↗ — opens the place in the Naver Map app. With the app (or your phone) set to English, visitor reviews are translated automatically. Install Naver Map first — it’s the app you’ll navigate with anyway.

The Korean name is always exact — it comes straight from Naver, and every one was tested to return the right restaurant. The English name is our best match to what Google shows; if you spot one that differs, tell us and we’ll fix it.

Three Korean phrases for eating alone

혼자예요hon-ja-ye-yo — “Just me.” Say it at the door; it answers the only question they have. 1인분 돼요?il-in-bun dwae-yo? — “Can I order one portion?” Useful at BBQ and stew places. 여기서 먹고 갈게요yeo-gi-seo meok-go gal-ge-yo — “For here.” Kiosks and counters will ask.

Most places on this list have counter or bar seating, kiosks with an English option, or single-serving menus — nobody will blink.

Why this exists

Google Maps barely works for navigation in Korea — everyone uses Naver Map or Kakao Map. But those apps only know restaurants by their Korean names. Search “Hadongkwan” on Naver and you get nothing; search 하동관 and everything appears: hours, photos, waiting times, thousands of reviews.

We tested it: typing the Google-style English name into Naver finds the right place first time in fewer than 1 in 5 cases. Typing the Korean name from this list: every time.

And solo dining? Some Korean restaurants turn away single diners (BBQ and stew places often require 2+ orders), and you can’t tell from Google — that information only lives in Korean-language reviews. So every place here was checked against Naver’s own visitor votes for “good for eating alone”. If it’s on the list, a table for one is fine.
